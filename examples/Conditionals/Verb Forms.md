## Verb Forms in Conditionals

To properly construct conditional sentences and manipulate hypothetical timelines, English relies on a specific set of verb forms and helper verbs.

### The Foundation Tenses
- **The Present Simple**: Used for describing unchanging facts or habits. It forms the realistic anchor for zero and first conditionals.
- **The Past Simple**: Used to talk about completed actions. In conditionals, shifting to the past simple signals that a situation is unreal or hypothetical.

### The Perfect Tenses
- **The Past Participle**: A building block form of a verb (often ending in -ed or -en) used to construct more complex tenses.
- **The Past Perfect**: Constructed using *had* + a **Past Participle**, it makes it clear that one event happened before another. In conditionals, it establishes impossible, unchangeable past scenarios.

### Modality
- **Modal Verbs**: Helper verbs (like *will*, *would*, *can*, *might*) that never change form. They are used to express the degree of certainty, hypothetical results, and projected possibilities in the main clause of a conditional sentence.
